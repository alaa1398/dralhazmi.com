## Key numbers
- `4 weeks` | stop smoking at least this long before surgery | guideline | R1
- `2–4 weeks` | low-calorie "liver-shrinking" diet before surgery [CHAIR] | guideline | R1
- `6 hours` | last light meal before anesthesia | guideline | R1, R4
- `2 hours` | last clear drink (water) before anesthesia | guideline | R1, R4
- `12–18 months` | wait before getting pregnant after surgery [CHAIR] | guideline | R2, R11

## Sections
### 1. Your checks before surgery
- Visit the surgeon to plan your operation — [guideline, R1]
- Meet the dietitian for a full nutrition check — [guideline, R1]
- Meet the psychologist to talk about habits and support — [guideline, R1]
- Have blood tests and other checks your doctors order — [guideline, R2]
- Answer sleep apnea questions (breathing stops during sleep) — [guideline, R1]
- You may need a camera test of the stomach (endoscopy) — [guideline, R8; CHAIR]

### 2. Stop smoking and vaping
- Stop all smoking at least 4 weeks before surgery — [guideline, R1]
- Smoking raises risk of ulcers, infection, lung problems — [guideline, R1]
- Stop vaping and all nicotine products too — [expert-opinion, R11]
- Ask your team for help to quit — [guideline, R1]

### 3. Diet and activity
- Follow a low-calorie diet for 2–4 weeks before surgery — [guideline, R1; CHAIR]
- It shrinks the liver, so surgery is safer — [guideline, R1]
- Diabetes? Your sugar can drop low; check it often — [guideline, R1]
- Keep active, for example with daily walks — [expert-opinion; R1: exercise programs unproven]

### 4. Medicines: tell your team about all of them
- Blood thinners: ask before stopping; never stop on your own — [expert-opinion]
- Diabetes medicines may need new doses before surgery — [guideline, R1]
- Weight-loss injections (GLP-1 medicines): ask early; a liquid diet may be needed — [guideline, R3; CHAIR]
- Herbal products and supplements: tell your team; some are paused — [consensus, R7]
- Hormone pills: your team may pause them before surgery — [guideline, R2 (wording not verified); R1: estrogen pills raise clot risk]

### 5. Pregnancy and birth control
- Avoid pregnancy for 12–18 months after surgery — [guideline, R2; R11; CHAIR]
- Fertility often rises after surgery; plan birth control now — [consensus, R5]
- Birth control pills may work less well after surgery — [consensus, R5]
- After either operation, ask about an implant or IUD (coil) — [consensus, R5]

### 6. Fasting and what to bring
- Last light meal 6 hours before anesthesia — [guideline, R1, R4]
- Clear drinks (water) until 2 hours before — [guideline, R1, R4]
- Follow the exact times your hospital gives you — [expert-opinion]
- Bring your sleep apnea (CPAP) machine and mask — [guideline, R1]
- Bring your medicines or a written list — [expert-opinion]
- Bring loose clothes, flat shoes, glasses, charger; leave valuables home — [expert-opinion]

## When to contact your surgical team
- You get a fever, cough, or cold before surgery — [expert-opinion]
- You start, stop, or change any medicine — [expert-opinion]
- You think you may be pregnant — [expert-opinion]
- You cannot follow the diet, or sugar readings stay low — [expert-opinion]

## Call 997 or go to the emergency department
- Chest pain, severe breathlessness, or fainting — [expert-opinion]

### Safety
- No emergency sign is understated for a pre-surgery sheet. The 997 panel lists only chest pain, breathlessness, fainting; adequate for this topic.
- SGLT2 inhibitors (for example, diabetes pills that must be stopped days before surgery) are not named. "Diabetes medicines may need new doses" covers this in general; Tariq should confirm whether a specific line is needed. I did not verify a hold time.
- GLP-1 item is safe as written ("ask early"); the hold or diet rule remains a [CHAIR] decision.

### Newer guidance found
- GLP-1: no US multi-society update newer than R3 (2024). Newer documents: SPAQI 2025 (R9) and ADS/ANZCA/GESA/NACOS 2025 (R10), which differ from R3 (R10: continue and 24-hour clear fluids for all; R3: continue for most, liquid diet only if higher risk).
