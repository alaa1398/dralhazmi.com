## Sections

### The stages
Your team tells you when to move on.
- Clear liquids: water, broth, decaf tea. First 1–2 days. — [guideline, R1, R2]
- Full liquids: high-protein, low-sugar drinks and soups. To week 1–2. — [guideline, R1]
- Puree (smooth) after liquids; soft (mash with a fork) from week 2–3. — [guideline, R1]
- Regular textures: from week 4–6. — [guideline, R1]
- Try one new food at a time. — [expert-opinion]

### Protein first
- At least 60 g protein a day. — [guideline, R1, R2]
- Eat the protein part of each meal first. — [expert-opinion]
- Spread protein over meals and snacks. — [guideline, R1]
- Eggs, fish, chicken, yogurt, cheese, lentils. — [guideline, R1]
- Milk upsets you? Choose lactose-free (milk-sugar-free). — [guideline, R1]

### Drink enough, by sipping
- Sip all day, between meals. — [guideline, R1]
- Aim for 6–8 cups a day. — [guideline, R1]
- Do not drink with meals. — [guideline, R2]
- After eating, wait 30 minutes before drinking. — [guideline, R3 via R4; consensus, R5 (for dumping)] [CHAIR]
- Limit caffeine (regular coffee and tea) early on. — [guideline, R1]

### Eating habits for life
- Small meals at set times; no grazing (nibbling all day). — [guideline, R1]
- Small bites. Chew each bite well. — [guideline, R1, R2]
- Eat slowly: 20–30 minutes per meal. — [guideline, R1, R2]
- Stop when comfortable, not full. — [guideline, R1]

### Avoid early on
- Fizzy (carbonated) drinks. — [guideline, R1]
- Sugary drinks, sweets, baked goods. — [guideline, R1; consensus, R5]
- Sugar can cause "dumping" (see below), more often after bypass. — [consensus, R5]
- Tough or dry meat. — [guideline, R1]
- Bread, rice, pasta: may feel "stuck" early. — [guideline, R1]
- Skins, peels, stringy vegetables. — [guideline, R1]

### Signs a food is not agreeing with you
- Nausea, vomiting, or food coming back up. — [guideline, R1]
- Food feels stuck after you swallow. — [guideline, R1]
- After sweets: sweating, fast heartbeat, dizziness, cramps, diarrhea ("dumping"). — [guideline, R1; consensus, R5]
- Bloating or diarrhea after milk. — [guideline, R1]
- Vomiting? Go back to liquids or soft food for 1–2 days. — [guideline, R1]

## When to contact your surgical team
- Vomiting that keeps coming back. — [guideline, R2]
- Dizziness or headache from too little fluid. — [guideline, R1]
- Food gets stuck again and again. — [expert-opinion]
- Dumping that diet changes do not fix. — [consensus, R5 statement 49]
- Before fasting Ramadan: plan it with your surgeon and dietitian. — [consensus, R6]

## Call 997 or go to the emergency department
- Vomit that is bright red or dark brown. — [guideline, R1]
- You cannot keep any fluids down. — [expert-opinion; R1 Table 16 advises ED check for dehydration]
- Severe belly pain, chest pain, or trouble breathing. — [expert-opinion]

### Claims changed (before → after)
- "Puree (smooth), then soft (mash with a fork): from week 2–3." → "Puree (smooth) after liquids; soft (mash with a fork) from week 2–3." R1 Table 11 says "A soft food stage typically begins 2–3 weeks after surgery" and gives no start week for puree.
- "Limit coffee and tea early on." → "Limit caffeine (regular coffee and tea) early on." R1 allows "caffeine-free tea and coffee" and says "Limit or avoid caffeine".
- Wait-30-minutes item: the tag now reads "R3 via R4; R5 (for dumping)", because R5 statement 44 applies only to patients with dumping.
- "Small meals at set times; no grazing" [R1, R3] → [R1]. R1: "Establishing an eating schedule to prevent grazing behaviors". I could not read R3.
- "Small bites. Chew each bite well." [R2, R3] → [R1, R2]. R1 Table 16: "Small bites, chew foods thoroughly, eat slowly".
- "Bypass especially: sugar can cause dumping" [guideline, R3] → "Sugar can cause dumping, more often after bypass" [consensus, R5]. R5 reports dumping after both operations. I could not read R3.
- "Food feels stuck in your chest" → "Food feels stuck after you swallow". R1 Table 16 says foods get "stuck" or "lodged in the throat".
- "Then: go back one stage for 1–2 days" → "Vomiting? Go back to liquids or soft food for 1–2 days." R1 Table 16, Vomiting row: "Return to full liquids or semi-soft foods for 1–2 days".
- "Vomiting that keeps coming back" [R1, R2] → [R2]. R1 has no matching instruction to contact the team. R2: thiamine risk "is further increased in the presence of vomiting ... it must be treated immediately".

### Confirmed as written (R1 unless noted)
- Clear liquids on post-op days 0–1, "should not last longer than 48 hours".
- Full liquids from day 2 "through 7–14 days", "high in protein, low in added sugar, lactose-free".
- Regular textures "4–6 weeks after surgery".
- Fluids "48–64 oz/day"; "Allowing 20–30 minutes for meals".
- "minimal protein intake of 60 g/d"; "spread out throughout the day".
- Protein foods (Table 12), lactose-free milk, and no carbonation.
- Foods to limit: "candies, baked goods, sugary drinks"; tough/dry meats and breads/pasta get "stuck"; avoid "skins, peels, fibrous components".
- "stop eating when comfortable".
- "If vomit is bright red or dark brown, patients should contact their medical provider immediately." Sending these patients to 997 is the practice's own escalation.
