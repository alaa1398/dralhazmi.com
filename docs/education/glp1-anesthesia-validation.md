# GLP-1 medicines before surgery: anesthesia guidance check (decision P3)

Date: 2026-11-27. Purpose: check Dr. Alhazmi's proposal that patients stop GLP-1 medicines (and dual GIP/GLP-1 medicines) **2 weeks before surgery** because of aspiration risk, against anesthesia society guidance. Source: cloud session, Tavily web search and extract (approved by the owner).

## Short answer

- **The concern is well founded.** GLP-1 medicines slow stomach emptying. A longer pause lowers the chance of food left in the stomach. One week is often not enough, and some studies found extra gastric volume even after 3 weeks off.
- **No anesthesia society guideline I could confirm sets 2 weeks as the standard stop time.** The US society uses 1 week for weekly injections. The newer UK, Australian–New Zealand and US multidisciplinary documents advise continuing the medicine and using a 24-hour clear-liquid diet instead, partly because a 1–2 week stop may not empty the stomach either.
- **One possible exception:** a 2026 review reports that the European anaesthesia society (ESAIC) 2025 guideline allows "up to two weeks in high-risk contexts such as obesity or bariatric surgery". I could not find or open that guideline, so **I cannot confirm this.**
- **Bariatric-specific point:** the UK consensus (written with the bariatric surgery and bariatric anaesthesia societies) notes that bariatric patients are usually already on a 2–4 week liquid-based liver-reduction diet before surgery.

## Evidence behind a 2-week stop

- Semaglutide's half-life is about 6–7 days; a review estimates that effects on stomach emptying "may persist for 2-4 weeks after discontinuation" [S9].
- A study presented at the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons 2025 meeting (joint replacement patients) found that stopping semaglutide 2 weeks before surgery lowered anesthesia-related complications, including aspiration [S10]. This is a conference abstract in a different surgical population, not a guideline.
- Against it: ANZCA judges a 1–2 week stop "unlikely to alter gastric emptying"; the UK consensus cites raised gastric volume even after 3 weeks off; stopping can worsen blood sugar in diabetes and bring back stomach side effects when the drug is restarted [S6, S7, S5].

## Revised options for P3

| Option | Supported by |
|---|---|
| a) Stop 2 weeks before surgery (your proposal) | Stomach-emptying physiology; one joint-replacement study; ESAIC 2025 as reported (unconfirmed). Not the standard in ASA, the US multi-society guidance, SPAQI, ANZCA or the UK consensus. |
| b) Stop on the day (daily) or 1 week before (weekly) | ASA 2023; the multi-society 2024 hold times for higher-risk patients. |
| c) Continue, with a 24-hour clear-liquid diet before surgery | SPAQI 2025, ANZCA 2025; the multi-society 2024 for higher-risk patients. |
| Any option: tell the anesthesia team, who decide on the day | All sources. |

Whatever you choose, the guide's current line ("Weight-loss injections (GLP-1 medicines): ask early; you may need a liquid diet first.") would need its wording changed only for options a) and b).
