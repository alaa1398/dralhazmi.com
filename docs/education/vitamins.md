## Sections

### Every day, for life
- Take a complete multivitamin with minerals every day. — [guideline, R1, R2]
- How many a day: your team will tell you. — [CHAIR 1]
- Early on, chewable or liquid forms are easier to take. — [guideline, R8 Table 11]
- Never stop on your own. Vitamins are for life. — [guideline, R2]
- Buy supplements only from a licensed pharmacy. — [expert-opinion]

### Calcium and vitamin D: for your bones
- Aim for 1,200–1,500 mg calcium a day, food plus supplements. — [guideline, R1 quoted in R2]
- Split calcium into smaller doses through the day. — [guideline, R1 quoted in R2]
- Calcium citrate: with or without food. Carbonate: with meals. — [guideline, R1 quoted in R2] [CHAIR 5]
- Take vitamin D daily; your blood test guides the dose. — [guideline, R1, R2] [CHAIR 2]

### Iron and vitamin B12: for your blood
- Many people need extra iron beyond the multivitamin. — [guideline, R2] [CHAIR 4]
- Women who have periods usually need more iron. — [guideline, R1, R2]
- Take iron with orange juice or vitamin C. — [guideline, R2]
- Keep iron and calcium at least 2 hours apart. — [guideline, R2, R8]
- You need extra vitamin B12: injection or daily tablet. — [guideline, R1, R2] [CHAIR 3]

### Vitamin B1 (thiamine): protects nerves and brain
- Your multivitamin must contain vitamin B1. — [guideline, R1, R2]
- B1 can drop fast if you keep vomiting. — [guideline, R2]
- Some teams add extra B1 for the first 3–4 months. — [guideline, R2] [CHAIR 6]

### Sleeve or bypass: what differs
- Most vitamin advice is the same for both operations. — [guideline, R2]
- After bypass, selenium (a mineral) is also checked. — [guideline, R2]
- After bypass, some people need extra vitamin A. — [guideline, R2]

### Your blood tests
- Year one: about 3, 6 and 12 months after surgery. — [guideline, R2, R3] [CHAIR 7]
- After that: at least once a year, for life. — [guideline, R2, R3]
- Blood count, iron stores (ferritin), folate (a B vitamin) and B12. — [guideline, R2]
- Vitamin D, calcium, kidney and liver tests. — [guideline, R2]
- Zinc and copper (minerals) at least once a year. — [guideline, R2]

## When to contact your surgical team
- Vomiting that does not stop, or you cannot keep vitamins down. — [guideline, R2]
- Eating very little, or losing weight very fast. — [guideline, R2]
- Numbness, tingling or burning in your feet or legs. — [guideline, R2]
- Hair loss or a change in taste. — [guideline, R2] [CHAIR 8]
- Unusual tiredness, breathlessness or pale skin. — [expert-opinion]
- You stopped your vitamins, for any reason. — [expert-opinion]

## Call 997 or go to the emergency department
- Confusion, or very sleepy and hard to wake. — [guideline, R2]
- Cannot walk steadily, or new trouble with balance. — [guideline, R2]
- Fast heartbeat with breathlessness or swollen legs. — [guideline, R2]
- Tell staff: "I had weight-loss surgery. Check vitamin B1." — [expert-opinion, based on R2]

### Confirmed as written (R2 unless noted)
- A complete multivitamin and mineral supplement daily after all procedures.
- Additional iron after sleeve or bypass, twice daily if menstruating; take it with citrus or vitamin C.
- Calcium in divided doses.
- B12 injections every 3 months.
- Vitamin D 2,000–4,000 IU, with a level of at least 75 nmol/L.
- Extra B1 can be considered for the first 3–4 months.
- Extra vitamin A may be needed after RYGB (Grade C). Selenium is checked after RYGB.
- Tests at 3, 6 and 12 months, then at least yearly: blood count, ferritin, folate, B12, vitamin D, calcium, renal and liver tests, and zinc and copper.
- Contact the team for "prolonged vomiting, dysphagia, poor nutritional intake, inability to tolerate vitamin and mineral supplements ... or fast weight loss" (§8.9).
- Symptoms of thiamine deficiency: "ataxia, confusion and coma ... neuropathy ... or cardiac insufficiency with tachycardia and respiratory symptom" (§7.6). These support the 997 lines.
- Emergency staff must avoid glucose before thiamine (§9.6). This supports the "tell staff" line.
