# Design panel: judges' scores and notes


## Mockup A · Quiet luxury editorial ("The Plate") (`a-quiet-luxury`)
- **Concept:** The homepage is treated as a printed monograph: warm paper, deep ink, one pine-teal accent, and the single portrait set as a square-edged plate with crop marks and a caption on a hairline. Hierarchy comes from scale contrast (a 92px display serif beside 12px small-caps labels), a 12-column grid and hairline rules, with no cards, shadows or icons. Every section opens with a numbered rule (01 to 06), sequences that really are ordered (journey steps, the 15 FAQs, treatments) get numerals, and the page closes on a full-width typographic colophon of the surgeon's name. Arabic is composed separately: Markazi Text display at larger sizes, IBM Plex Sans Arabic body at 1.85 leading, no tracking, no italics, logical properties throughout. All copy is taken programmatically from site.ts and index.astro, so the approved wording is unchanged.
- **Typography:** English: Newsreader variable with the optical-size axis for display (weight 400, -0.022em tracking, display clamp 2.6rem to 5.75rem, h2 up to 3.9rem); static Newsreader 400 italic only for the pull quote; Inter 400/500 for body (17-18px, 1.62 leading) and tracked uppercase labels (12.5px, 0.15em). Arabic: Markazi Text variable (500) for headings at about 1.25-1.6x the Latin size with 1.08-1.2 leading; IBM Plex Sans Arabic 400/600 for body (18-19px, 1.85 leading); labels are semibold with no tracking or uppercase; Latin tokens (FACS, credentials, phone) are isolated in bdi/dir=ltr and set in Inter or Markazi Latin. A global :lang(ar) reset forces letter-spacing 0, no text-transform and no font synthesis. text-wrap balance on headings, pretty on paragraphs. The Arabic switch word on English pages uses a system face so no Arabic webfont is downloaded there.
- **Palette:** Paper #F8F5EF, sand #EFEAE0 (alternate sections, BMI panel, footer), ink #15202A (headings and the one dark band), body #36414B, muted #5A646D, a single accent pine-teal #0D5C55 (hover #094740) used for the filled WhatsApp button, one headline line, section numbers and journey numerals; accent tint #9FD6CB on the dark band only. Hairlines are ink at 16% and 55%. Contrast was chosen for AA by calculation (accent on paper about 7:1, muted on sand about 4.7:1) but axe was not run.
- **Signature moves:** Portrait as a printed plate: 4:5 square-edged crop, corner crop marks, caption (name, title, post-nominals) on a hairline; the same crop marks frame the closing second-opinion block and the inner-page contact box; Numbered section rules: every section opens with a full-width hairline and a small-caps label (01 Robotic Surgery, 02 Two paths to treatment ...); on Treatments the h2 itself is the label and the approved intro sentence is set at display size; Treatments as an oversized typographic index: number, 50px serif name, summary, arrow, separated by hairlines; the risk paragraph sits under the summary column at full body size, not as fine print; Journey as four large accent numerals (CSS counters) hanging from one hairline with dots; turns vertical on mobile and runs right-to-left in Arabic; Single dark ink band for robotics with one abstract line drawing (concentric arcs and two instrument-path lines), reused on the treatment page and mirrored in RTL; FAQ: sticky side heading, 15 native details rows with counter numerals, plus/minus on the inline-end, sources kept inside each answer; the 'Medically reviewed · Updated' line is the section's dateline; Full-width colophon footer: the name at up to 184px, then phone, Faisal AI-assistant disclosure and the 997 emergency note in normal-size text; Faisal disclosure placed directly under the phone number in the hero, closing block, footer and treatment contact box; Contact is the only filled colour: a floating WhatsApp pill on desktop, a WhatsApp + Call bar on mobile with reserved bottom padding and scroll-padding
- **Inner pages plan:** treatment.html shows the inner-page template: breadcrumb in small caps, display h1, the summary as a serif standfirst, an optional ruled fact strip (used here with the four approved robotic points; dropped where no approved facts exist), then numbered chapters with a sticky side heading (01 What it is, 02 Who it may suit, 03 What to expect), a crop-marked contact box with phone, Faisal note and WhatsApp button, the disclaimer and review line, and the remaining treatments as the typographic index with the risk note. About: the plate portrait once at small size, the bio as chapters, credentials as the hairline dl already on the homepage. Journey: the numeral steps expanded to full chapters, one per step. Second opinion: the closing crop-marked block promoted to the page hero, followed by chapters; still asks for a question only. Resources/Learn: the typographic index pattern with source name, description and external arrow. Privacy: a single 65-character reading column with chapter numerals and no decoration. All pages share the header, numbered section rule, colophon footer and contact bar; the arcs drawing is limited to robotic content.
- **New font packages:** @fontsource-variable/newsreader, @fontsource-variable/markazi-text
- **Page weight:** Measured by recording every file Chromium actually requested (uncompressed bytes on disk; html and css would be smaller gzipped). index.html: 279 KB at 1440px (1x), 294 KB at 390px (3x, larger portrait); with gzip on html/css about 229 / 244 KB. Breakdown: Newsreader opsz variable 132 KB, Newsreader italic 24 KB, Inter 400+500 48 KB, CSS 36 KB, HTML 32 KB, portrait 14 or 29 KB. ar.html: 286 KB at 1440px, 301 KB at 390px (about 229 / 244 KB with gzip): Markazi Text Arabic 41 KB + Latin 26 KB, Plex Arabic 400+600 89 KB, Inter 48 KB, CSS 36 KB, HTML 40 KB, portrait. treatment.html: 221 KB. Baseline for comparison is about 250 KB English and 470 KB Arabic, so Arabic drops by roughly 170 KB and English rises by roughly 30-45 KB.
- **Designer's self-critique:** Not verified: axe was not run, so "0 violations" is unconfirmed; contrast was calculated by hand, not measured. I did not test keyboard order, 200% zoom, 375px exactly (the helper checks 390px, no overflow reported), Firefox or Safari. The English page is heavier than the baseline because the Newsreader optical-size file alone is 132 KB; that buys the finer display cut, but the static 22 KB files would look nearly as good and save about 85 KB. The hero accent line is roman, not italic, because the italic I ship is the text cut and mismatched the display roman; the baseline's italic line had charm that this loses. Markazi Text is light and modern but reads plainer than Amiri at hero size, and its Latin numerals (15+, FACS, 01-04) look sturdier than the Newsreader figures on the English page, so the two languages are not perfectly matched; a native Arabic reader should judge the Arabic headings. The hero is still text-left, portrait-right with a three-cell strip, which is refined, not a new composition. Section labels 03 and 06 behave differently from the others (h2 as label; review dateline as label), which is a small inconsistency. The mobile nav wraps to two rows instead of a designed menu. The desktop floating WhatsApp pill can overlap content at the bottom corner (it covered the Arabic portrait caption's end in one screenshot). The 15 FAQs are not grouped by topic because that would need new headings. I added the approved Faisal note and site title in new positions (hero, closing block, portrait caption) and a few aria-labels in both languages (navigation, breadcrumb, footer), which the owner should confirm. Inner links other than index/ar/treatment are '#'. The pages are generated by a small Node script outside the deliverable folder; porting to Astro is straightforward but not done. With more time: run axe and a keyboard pass, try the static Newsreader files and Amiri-vs-Markazi side by side, design the mobile menu, and build Arabic treatment and About pages.


## Mockup b-precision-dark — "Precision" (`b-precision-dark`)
- **Concept:** The site is treated as a calibrated instrument: the one real portrait sits inside a ticked dial with a single luminous teal arc, and that vocabulary (tick rulers, concentric arcs, an instrument-path curve) recurs as the only decoration. Deep ink sections (hero, robotic, journey, closing call, footer) carry the cinematic moments, while warm paper and sand sections carry everything that has to be read: paths, treatments, risks, about and the FAQ. Data is set in monospace (phone number, indices, the BMI read-out on a mirrored scale) and headlines in a light serif, so the page feels exact but still warm. Arabic is composed separately, with a large Markazi Text display, IBM Plex Sans Arabic body, no tracking or italics, and a mirrored layout in which the portrait, digits and Latin credentials stay unmirrored. All approved copy, the 15 sourced FAQs, the Faisal disclosure, the 997 note and the Contact-only calls to action are reproduced verbatim from the repo.
- **Typography:** English: Newsreader 300 for display (h1 up to 5.5rem, tracking -0.028em) and 400 for small serif headings; Inter 400/500 for body at 17px/1.6; IBM Plex Mono 400 for eyebrows, indices, the phone number and BMI values with tabular figures. Arabic: Markazi Text variable at weight 500 for display (h1 up to 8.6rem, sized up because the face is compact); IBM Plex Sans Arabic 400/600 for body at 18px/1.85 and for labels (SemiBold, no tracking, no uppercase); Latin digits and tokens fall through to Plex Mono, Inter and Newsreader. A global :lang(ar) reset forces letter-spacing 0 and text-transform none; font-synthesis is off; emphasis is by accent colour only, never italic, in both languages. All sizes are rem + vw clamps with max at most 2.5x min. Amiri is dropped.
- **Palette:** Ink #06141B and raised ink #0B1F29 for dark surfaces; paper #F6F4EF and sand #ECE8DF for reading surfaces. One accent in two tunings: luminous teal #62E6D6 on dark only, and deep teal #0A6763 on paper for text and links. Text on dark is #EAF2F3 with #A9BEC3 secondary; on light it is #06141B, #2A3B43 body and #53656C muted. Hairlines are 16% and 30-40% tints of the text colour. The only filled, high-contrast element on any screen is the teal Contact / WhatsApp button. axe-core reports 0 violations on all three pages.
- **Signature moves:** Portrait dial: the single photo in a circular mask inside an SVG ring of 180 calibrated ticks, crosshair stubs and one teal arc that draws once on load; the credentials (MD · FACS · ABS · ABOM · MBA) run along the ring as curved mono text. In RTL the ring decoration mirrors, but the face and the Latin text do not.; Tick-ruler hairlines: every major rule (stats strip, robotic points, BMI panel, footer, treatment meta strip) carries a row of 1px ticks, so dividers read as a measuring scale instead of boxes.; BMI calculator as an instrument: dark panel, mono inputs, a large teal read-out and a 15-45 ruler with the 25/30/35 guideline thresholds and a glowing marker. The scale mirrors in RTL and the original logic, including Arabic-Indic digits and the Arabic decimal separator, is kept (tested with ١٧٢ / ٩٨٫٥).; Sticky mono rail: each section has a '01 / 06' index and name in a narrow start column that sticks while content scrolls; the FAQ heading sticks beside its 15 questions. It collapses to an inline label under 68rem.; Treatments as a typographic index: five full-width rows with mono number, large serif name, summary and a circular arrow that mirrors in RTL; the risk note follows directly as a full-contrast bordered callout, not small print.; Journey as an instrument path: one fine sine curve through four teal nodes with large serif counters 01-04, flipped for RTL and turned into a vertical line on mobile.; FAQ without boxes: native details in three groups, mono counters 01-15, plus/minus on the inline-end, 17-18px answers on a 48rem measure, and a dashed-rule Sources list per answer; details expand in print.; Four abstract line glyphs for the robotic points (stereo lens, wristed joint with angle arc, small marks against a long dashed line, four nodes on a path) and an abstract access figure on the treatment page (entry points on an arc converging on a crosshair); nothing depicts a robot or anatomy.; Footer colophon with the name at display size, then phone in mono with the Faisal disclosure and 997 note at normal contrast. The Faisal line also sits directly under the phone number in the hero and the closing call.; Contact stays calm and constant: teal WhatsApp pill in the hero, a floating WhatsApp pill on desktop, and a 2-button bottom bar on mobile with reserved body padding and scroll-padding.
- **Inner pages plan:** treatment.html shows the inner-page template: a compact dark page hero (mono breadcrumb, large serif h1, summary as lead, one abstract line figure, tick-ruled meta strip with the review line), then a paper reading area of hairline-separated rows with a sticky mono index (01 / 03) and 18-21px text, a dark contact box (phone in mono, Faisal note, WhatsApp button), the disclaimer and review line, and a compact index of the other treatments.

- **About:** same page hero with the small circular portrait and credentials; biography in the reading rows; education, fellowship and certification as the ruled facts list; past KFSHRC experience as a plain dated text row, never a logo.
- **Treatments index:** the homepage typographic list at full size, robotic first, followed by the risk callout.
- **Other treatment pages:** same template, each with its own small abstract figure; the bariatric page adds its ASMBS source in the Sources style used in the FAQ.
- **Journey:** the instrument-path steps expanded vertically, one row per step with large counters, closing with the contact box.
- **Second opinion:** the closing-call composition as the hero, then numbered rows; wording stays question-only, with no request for reports.
- **Resources / Learn / Links:** ruled link lists with mono source labels and external-link arrows.
- **Privacy:** plain reading rows with the sticky index as a table of contents; no decoration beyond the page-hero rule.
- **404 and language redirect:** dark page hero only.

For Astro, the tokens and components map directly onto global.css and Base.astro. The mockup was generated from site.ts and index.astro by a small script, so no copy was retyped.
- **New font packages:** @fontsource/ibm-plex-mono, @fontsource-variable/markazi-text
- **Page weight:** Measured as the sum of bytes of every file Chromium actually loaded at 1440px, DPR 1, uncompressed, with zero external requests.

- **index.html (English):** 200.7 KB (HTML 34 + CSS 47 + five fonts 108 + portrait-480.webp 11). About 216 KB on 2x screens, which load the 26 KB portrait-880. With gzip on HTML and CSS it is about 138 KB on the wire.
- **ar.html (Arabic):** 339.4 KB (HTML 43 + CSS 47 + eight fonts 238 + portrait 11). About 355 KB on 2x screens; about 269 KB with gzip.
- **treatment.html:** 165.6 KB.

Baseline for comparison is about 250 KB English and 470 KB Arabic.
- **Designer's self-critique:** Checked: 0 axe violations on all three pages, no horizontal overflow at 375, 390 and 1440, BMI tested with Western and Arabic-Indic digits, mobile menu and FAQ open states screenshotted. Not checked: Firefox or Safari, 200%/400% zoom, a keyboard-only pass, and animated states (the helper shoots with reduced motion on).

Weaknesses:
- **Dark is a real risk for this audience.** Roughly 45% of the scroll is near-black. I kept all long reading on paper, but a jury member who wants a calm clinic feel may find the hero and journey more tech than care. The only warmth comes from the portrait, the serif and the paper tone.
- **Newsreader is the static text cut, not the optical-size display cut.** The variable opsz file is 132 KB, so I used weight 300 with tight tracking; the display cut would look finer at 88px.
- **Arabic h1 is very large and heavy** (Markazi 500 at about 138px), which pushes the stats strip below the first desktop viewport on the Arabic page. Markazi sizing was tuned by eye from screenshots only and needs a native reader's review, as does the diacritic clearance on words like بدقّة.
- **The Arabic page still loads four Latin font files** (about 85 KB) for digits, acronyms and citations; subsetting those to digits and capitals would cut most of that.
- **The language-switch word "العربية" on the English page uses a system font**, to avoid downloading 43 KB of Plex Arabic for one word. In the screenshots it renders in DejaVu; production should ship a tiny subset.
- **A few layout-only labels are new and need owner approval:** FAQ group headings (Before a decision / Life after surgery / Practical matters), "Sourced answers", "Sources", "Risks" and "Menu", with Arabic equivalents. No medical wording was changed.
- **The curved credentials text on the dial is about 10px on desktop.** It is decorative and aria-hidden (the credentials appear as real text elsewhere), but it is small.
- **On mobile the portrait sits below the fold**, under the copy and call to action; only its top edge shows in the first screen.
- **Mixed arrows:** the approved "→ / ←" characters in the journey and about links are kept as text beside the SVG arrows used elsewhere.
- **The paths section has dead space** under the two lists on desktop, beside the taller BMI panel.
- **CSS is 47 KB unminified** (about 10 KB gzipped) and has some per-language overrides that would be tidier as tokens.
- **Inner-page links are "#" placeholders**, except the home/Arabic switch and the Robotic Surgery row.

With more time I would subset the fonts, test the optical-size display cut, add a per-treatment figure set, tune the Arabic hero scale with a native typographer, and run the cross-browser, zoom and keyboard passes.

- The mockup is produced by a generator script that reads the real copy from the repo, so wording is verbatim, but nothing has been ported to Astro components. Only "Home", the language switch and Robotic Surgery link anywhere; other links are "#".

## Design research principles


### medical-benchmarks
- Limits of this research: the sandbox proxy blocked browser access to external sites, so I read every exemplar as page text and structure, not as pixels. Colour, motion and typeface facts come from Awwwards listings and agency case studies and are cited as such. Sizes and ratios below are my recommendations unless a source is named.
- Keep the current direction and refine it. The baseline (warm off-white, deep ink, one teal accent, Newsreader serif over Inter, arch-masked portrait) already matches what the strongest references do: Odyssée runs on two colours and the Harley Street system is serif plus sans on warm stone. The gap is rhythm, illustration and Arabic polish, not a new concept.
- Colour: hold to 2 base colours plus 1 accent, with roughly 85-90% of each viewport neutral. Use the accent only for the eyebrow, links, one emphasised phrase per heading and focus rings. Allow one full-bleed dark ink band per page at most. Odyssée's entire listed palette is #1E305E on #F6F4F2.
- Hero: an H1 of 3-7 words, one supporting sentence of 25 words or fewer, then one primary action with the phone number as a text link beside it. West, Neko, Li Jiang and The London Clinic all do this. The current hero already complies, so keep it and do not add a carousel, video or second headline.
- Portrait: use the single photo large once (hero) and small once (about or footer signature). Keep it in a consistent mask and treat it tonally so it reads as art direction, not a headshot. Do not repeat it at full size on inner pages; let typography and illustration carry them.
- Credentials: use a ruled strip of 3-4 cells directly under the hero, each cell a short token in the display serif (FACS, 15+, 2 boards) with one plain line explaining what it means for a patient. West and Li Jiang both use 4 cells. Elsewhere put post-nominals on one quiet line, and list professional bodies as hairline-separated text, never as logos.
- Build trust without testimonials through four devices seen in the references: pull quotes in the surgeon's own voice that show restraint (West); a "Medically reviewed by ... · Last updated" line and a numbered sources list (Bassas); honest uncertainty in the journey copy (Li Jiang); and stating downsides next to benefits (Cleveland Clinic London). Use only wording that already exists in the approved copy.
- Replace missing photography with one bespoke line-illustration system: a 1.5px single-weight stroke in ink with the accent for the highlighted part. Draw one small icon per treatment and one larger numbered anatomical diagram per procedure page (stomach for sleeve, pouch and limb for bypass, port positions for robotic access), with labels 01-05 linked to a numbered list beside it as West does. Inline SVG, under about 3 KB each.
- Add one recurring abstract motif and use it at most 3 times per page (hero frame, section divider, footer). The arch outline already behind the portrait is the obvious candidate. Odyssée does this with a single sphere and Li Jiang with a spiral.
- Treatment pages: a 4-fact strip at the top (for example time in theatre, anaesthetic, hospital stay, return to activity), but only where approved copy already supplies those facts. Then follow the Cleveland Clinic order: who it is for, how it works, benefits and risks side by side, what to expect, recovery. Do not invent figures.
- Treatments index: lead with patient situations or conditions before procedure names. Use 2 featured cards and a compact ruled list for the rest (West), instead of a uniform grid of 6-9 identical cards.
- Patient journey: 3-5 numbered steps with large serif numerals (01, 02, 03), one short paragraph each, joined by a single hairline. Vertical on mobile, horizontal on desktop. No icon and no card chrome per step. Li Jiang uses 3 steps and Juneja uses 4.
- FAQ with 15 items: native details/summary, grouped under 3-4 topic headings, with a hairline between rows and no boxes. Question in the sans at 18-20px medium, answer at 16-17px with a 60-70 character measure. Show sources as superscript numbers resolving to one numbered list at the end of the section. Keep the plus/minus indicator on the inline-end side so it mirrors in RTL.
- Contact: one channel, repeated calmly. Put a WhatsApp button in the hero, a contact band at the end of every page and, on mobile only, a sticky bottom bar 56-64px high. Put the Faisal AI-assistant disclosure in normal-size text directly under the phone number wherever it appears, not in a footnote. No forms.
- Latin type: display serif at clamp(2.5rem, 5.5vw, 4.5rem) with line-height 1.05-1.1 and letter-spacing -0.01em. H2 at clamp(1.75rem, 3vw, 2.75rem). Body 17-18px with line-height 1.6 and a 60-72 character measure. Eyebrow 12-13px uppercase with 0.12-0.14em tracking. Use italic serif for exactly one emphasised phrase per page.
- Arabic type, using North's published numbers for IBM Plex Sans Arabic: 1.08x the Latin size for the same role, line-height 1.8 for body and 1.35 for headings, letter-spacing 0 everywhere. Eyebrows become SemiBold with no tracking and no uppercase. Replace italic emphasis with the accent colour or a weight change. Set text-underline-offset to 0.35em and font-synthesis to none. Check every headline in both languages side by side.
- RTL layout: use logical properties only (margin-inline, padding-inline, inset-inline, text-align: start). Mirror directional arrows and the journey direction. Do not mirror the portrait, phone numbers or Latin post-nominals; wrap numbers and FACS-type tokens in bdi or dir=ltr. Compose the Arabic hero in its own right, with the portrait on the inline-end, not as a flipped English layout.
- Arabic page weight: 470 KB against 250 KB for English is the clearest performance gap. Load at most 2 weights of each Arabic family, subset them, preload only the one face used above the fold, and consider Amiri for display sizes only.
- Section rhythm: one idea per section and one short H2 (Neko). Vertical padding of 96-144px on desktop and 56-72px on mobile, a 12-column grid with a 1120-1200px maximum width, and hairline rules (1px at about 12-15% ink) instead of card shadows. Alternate at most two background tones.
- Motion: CSS only. Fade and rise reveals of 12-16px over 400-600ms with an ease-out curve, once per element, staggered by 60-80ms. Hover transitions of 150-200ms. Optional cross-page View Transitions. No parallax, WebGL, scroll-jacking, marquees or counting numbers. Under prefers-reduced-motion, show everything immediately with no transform. Award sites reach this feel with GSAP, but it is not needed here.
- Self-identification before procedures, which suits the dual surgery and obesity-medicine positioning. The existing "Not every patient needs surgery" block already takes this approach. Strengthen it with 4-6 "where are you now" cards in the manner of Juneja, each linking to the relevant treatment or journey page, built from approved wording only.

Pitfalls:
- Copying award-site spectacle such as 3D scenes, WebGL, scroll-driven flights and custom cursors (Dr Grigoriak, Sky Clinics, Vibrant Wellness). It breaks the no-heavy-JavaScript and privacy rules, hurts accessibility and reads as cosmetic-clinic luxury, not surgical credibility.
- Importing the trust devices nearly every reference relies on but this site must not use: patient testimonials and Doctify or Google reviews (West, Li Jiang, Patel, The London Clinic), before/after sliders (West, Grigoriak), prices, packages and instalment offers (Li Jiang, Bassas), and "first follow-up visit free" (Bassas).
- Superlatives and outcome counters, which are common on Gulf surgeon sites: "Leading Surgeon in Dubai", "one of the most respected", "98%", "9,000 surgeries", "2900 satisfied patients". These break the Ministry of Health rules. Keep the credential strip to verifiable qualifications and years of experience.
- Animated count-up numbers. Juneja's and Valsamidis's stats render as "0+" until JavaScript runs. Render numbers statically.
- Asking for personal or clinical data in the contact prompt. Bassas asks for name, age, weight and height by WhatsApp. This site must ask for a question only and never for reports.
- Any "book", "appointment" or "consultation booking" wording carried over from reference patterns. Calls to action stay "Contact" / "Contact on WhatsApp" (تواصل).
- A dark, moody, near-black palette (Grigoriak, the Hallström template). It suits aesthetic surgery but makes long sourced medical text and Arabic body copy harder to read, and makes AA contrast for the teal accent harder to hold.
- Logo walls of hospitals, societies or "as seen on" media. Past KFSHRC experience must not look like a current affiliation, and logos must not be invented. Use plain text lists.
- Stock or AI imagery of patients, operating rooms or robots to fill space, and any illustration that resembles a specific vendor's robot. Keep illustrations anatomical and abstract.
- Treating Arabic as a mirrored English page: tracked or uppercase-style eyebrows, synthetic italics, line-height below 1.7, body text below 16px, faux bold, physical left/right CSS, or leaving the Arabic page almost twice as heavy as the English one.
- Card overload: boxing every item with shadows and icons. The strongest references use hairlines, numerals and whitespace, and feature 1-2 items instead of a uniform grid.
- Rewriting approved medical copy, FAQs or credentials to fit a new layout, for example shortening an answer to fit a card or inventing 4-fact strip values. Where approved copy has no fact for a slot, drop the slot.
- A sticky mobile contact bar that covers content or focus targets. Reserve bottom padding equal to its height, keep it out of the tab order until scrolled past the hero call to action, and check focus is not obscured (WCAG 2.2, 2.4.11).
- Present-tense or location-implying phrasing borrowed from references, such as "where he operates", clinic lists, maps and hours. No current workplace may be stated, and robotic practice stays in the past tense.


### editorial-craft
- CONTEXT FROM THE BASELINE I VIEWED (/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-dralhazmi-com/303fe75a-ef20-5ba6-9a6e-8876fccac785/scratchpad/redesign/baseline/ar-desktop-seg01.png): the current hero is already a competent editorial layout (Amiri display headline with one teal line, arch-masked portrait, pill CTA, hairline-ruled stat row '15+ / 2 / FACS'). The gap to 'stunning' is craft and distinctiveness, not a missing framework: scale contrast is timid, every element is centred on the same safe grid, and the arch-photo plus three-stat row is a very common template. Push typography and composition; do not add modules.
- TYPE SCALE: define 6-8 steps with clamp() using rem + vw only (Utopia: 360px to 1240px viewport, base 18px to 20px, ratio 1.2 to 1.25). Keep body nearly fixed (1.125rem to 1.25rem). For any step, max must be <= 2.5x min so WCAG 1.4.4 200% zoom still passes; never set font-size in px on html. A display hero step of about clamp(2.75rem, 1.6rem + 5vw, 6.5rem) is the upper bound that still satisfies the 2.5x rule; test at 200% and 400% zoom at 1280px wide.
- SCALE CONTRAST IS THE EFFECT: use only 3 visible sizes per viewport (display, body, small meta) with a jump of at least 3x between display and body. Editorial sites look expensive because of one very large element next to quiet 14-15px meta, not because of many mid-sized headings. Limit to 2 weights per family (Newsreader 400 + 500 or 600; Inter 400 + 500).
- OPTICAL SIZING: Newsreader has wght and opsz axes (per danburzo.ro/variable-fonts: 9 masters, 2 files, about 455 KB total with both axes; pinning opsz roughly halves a file, 215 KB to 98 KB). font-optical-sizing: auto is the default in all modern browsers, so the display cut appears automatically at large sizes only if the shipped file keeps the opsz axis. Check which @fontsource package is imported: if it is a static or opsz-less build, large headings are being drawn with the text cut and look heavy. Either ship the variable file with opsz for headings (subset to Latin, roman + italic only if italic is used) or accept the text cut and tighten tracking (-0.015em to -0.025em) above about 40px. Budget: keep the English page under roughly 300 KB total.
- LINE BREAKING: h1-h3, pull quotes, card titles, FAQ summaries get text-wrap: balance (supported everywhere current; Chromium balances up to 4 lines). Paragraphs and list items get text-wrap: pretty (Chrome 117+, Safari 26+, no Firefox; harmless fallback). Add hanging-punctuation: first on blockquotes inside @supports (Safari only, 17.4+). No hand-inserted <br> in translated strings.
- OPTICAL ALIGNMENT: use text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic on display headings, numerals and button labels so type sits exactly on hairlines and aligns with the portrait top edge (Chrome 133+, Safari 18.2+, Firefox 154+ per caniuse.com/css-text-box-trim). Wrap in @supports (text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic) and reduce the padding there, because untrimmed browsers need the original padding. For Arabic do not use cap; use text-box-edge: text or skip trimming, since Arabic has no cap height and marks will collide.
- MEASURE AND LEADING: Latin body 60-72ch (Butterick's range is 45-90 characters), line-height 1.5-1.6 for Inter body, 1.05-1.15 for display. Arabic body: size 1.1-1.15x the Latin size (IBM Plex Sans Arabic looks smaller at equal px), line-height 1.8-1.9, display Amiri 1.3-1.45 because of marks like the shadda visible on the current headline, measure about 55-65 characters. Set these with :lang(ar) overrides, not a single shared scale.
- ARABIC IS ITS OWN DESIGN: letter-spacing: 0 everywhere under :lang(ar) (the uppercase tracked eyebrow style must not be inherited), no italics, no text-transform, no synthetic bold (font-synthesis: none). Emphasis by weight or colour only. Use CSS logical properties throughout (margin-inline, padding-inline, inset-inline, border-inline-start, text-align: start) so nothing is mirrored by hand. Keep phone numbers and '+966 50 970 4040' in <bdi dir="ltr"> or unicode-bidi: isolate. Pick one numeral system site-wide (the baseline uses Western digits; keep it) and set font-variant-numeric: lining-nums tabular-nums on the stat row. Mirror directional arrows only; never mirror the WhatsApp glyph, check marks or the monogram.
- ARABIC DISPLAY OPPORTUNITY: Amiri is a Naskh with true calligraphic rhythm; at 5-7rem it is more striking than any Latin serif on the page. Let the Arabic hero headline be larger than the English one, give it room above for marks, and use it for one large pull quote per page. Do not try to justify Arabic body text or insert tatweel characters for stretching (W3C: tatweel in source breaks reflow, search and copy).
- COLOUR IN OKLCH WITH TINTED NEUTRALS: define one hue for the brand (the current teal is about hue 195-200) and build every neutral from the same hue at very low chroma, e.g. paper oklch(0.985 0.004 95) warm, ink oklch(0.22 0.02 240), rules color-mix(in oklch, var(--ink) 14%, transparent). Derive hover and pressed states with relative colour: oklch(from var(--accent) calc(l - 0.06) c h). oklch, color-mix and relative colours are in all current browsers; keep a hex fallback line before each for older Safari/Samsung. One accent only, used on under 5% of the surface. Check every pair at 4.5:1 (3:1 for text 24px and larger) since oklch lightness does not guarantee contrast.
- OPTIONAL DARK THEME: color-scheme: light dark plus light-dark(a, b) tokens (Chrome 123+, Firefox 120+, Safari 17.5+, Samsung 27+). Only worth doing if every token is defined twice and axe is re-run in both; otherwise set color-scheme: light explicitly so form controls and scrollbars do not flip.
- LAYOUT: one 12-column grid on the page wrapper; sections use grid-template-columns: subgrid (Chrome 117+, Firefox 71+, Safari 16+) so headings, numerals and body in different sections share the same column lines. Break symmetry on purpose: body copy in columns 5-11, section label hanging in columns 1-3, at least one element per page that crosses the grid (the display headline or a large numeral). Vary vertical rhythm: section padding alternating around 6rem and 10rem instead of one constant value.
- STICKY SIDE LABELS: on desktop each long section (treatments, journey, FAQ, risks) gets its heading or a 'No. 02 / Journey' label in the narrow start column with position: sticky; inset-block-start: 6rem; align-self: start, while content scrolls beside it. Pure CSS, works RTL automatically with logical properties. Requires no ancestor with overflow: hidden (use overflow: clip on the body if needed to stop horizontal overflow). Collapse to a normal heading under about 900px.
- LARGE NUMERALS FOR REAL SEQUENCES ONLY: the patient journey steps and the 15 FAQs are genuinely ordered, so set their numbers as display figures (Newsreader, 4-7rem, lining figures, accent or 30% ink) via CSS counters: counter-reset on the list, counter(step, decimal-leading-zero) in ::before. For Arabic keep the same digits as the body. Do not add counters or invented metrics anywhere else; the stat row may only show the existing verified facts.
- PULL QUOTES: one per long page maximum, taken verbatim from already-approved copy (never a patient quote, never a new claim). Set in Newsreader italic for English or Amiri regular for Arabic at step 3-4, text-wrap: balance, max 28ch, hanging in the wider grid column with a single hairline above. Mark the duplicate with aria-hidden="true" if it repeats body text so screen readers do not hear it twice.
- HAIRLINES INSTEAD OF BOXES: group with spacing and 1px rules (block-start border on rows) rather than bordered, shadowed, rounded cards. Use at most two radii in the whole system (for example 0 for editorial blocks and full pill for the Contact button, plus the arch on the portrait) and at most one shadow, reserved for the sticky header or mobile contact bar.
- FAQ ACCORDION WITHOUT JS: native <details>/<summary>, one row per question with a hairline, a plus/minus drawn in CSS, number in the start column. Animate with details::details-content { block-size: 0; overflow: clip; transition: block-size .3s, content-visibility .3s allow-discrete } and [open]::details-content { block-size: auto } inside @supports (interpolate-size: allow-keywords) with :root { interpolate-size: allow-keywords }. ::details-content is Chrome 131+, Firefox 143+, Safari 18.4+; interpolate-size is Chromium and Samsung 28+ only, so Safari and Firefox snap open, which is fine. Do not use the name attribute to make it exclusive: patients compare answers and the sources must stay reachable; also Ctrl+F find-in-page opens closed details only in Chromium.
- MOTION BUDGET: at most three motions on the whole site. (1) A 2px reading-progress rule on long pages using animation-timeline: scroll(root block) with transform: scaleX and transform-origin: inline-start. (2) One hero entrance, 400-600ms, opacity plus 8-12px translate, time-based, once. (3) Cross-document view transition. Everything else is static. Hover/focus transitions 120-180ms on colour and underline only.
- SCROLL-DRIVEN ANIMATION RULES: content must be fully visible with no CSS animation support. Write the visible state as the default and put the animation inside @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) { @supports (animation-timeline: view()) { ... } } using animation: reveal linear both; animation-timeline: view(); animation-range: entry 0% entry 35%. Declare animation-timeline after the animation shorthand (the shorthand resets it). Animate only opacity and transform. Support: Chrome 115+, Safari 26+, Firefox 159+; iOS 18 and older users simply see static content. Never apply reveal to every block; at most to large numerals or section rules.
- VIEW TRANSITIONS BETWEEN PAGES: add @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) { @view-transition { navigation: auto; } } in global.css. Keep the default root crossfade but shorten it: ::view-transition-old(root), ::view-transition-new(root) { animation-duration: 180ms }. Give the header view-transition-name: site-header so it stays still. Do not name the portrait or headings unless the name is unique per page (duplicate names cancel the transition). Chrome 126+, Safari 18.2+; Firefox and Samsung fall back to normal navigation. Note the EN/AR language switch is also same-origin, so it will crossfade too, which is desirable.
- DECORATION WITHOUT PHOTOS: choose one abstract motif and use it everywhere at different scales, for example the arch already used for the portrait, or fine concentric arcs / a precise 1px line drawing suggesting an instrument path. Inline SVG, stroke: currentColor, stroke-width 1, vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke, aria-hidden="true" focusable="false". No labelled diagrams of organs or robots, no vendor-shaped silhouettes, no figures of people.
- GRAIN AND GRADIENT: optional paper grain as a fixed 200x200 SVG data-URI tile (feTurbulence fractalNoise, baseFrequency 0.65-0.9, numOctaves 2-3, stitchTiles='stitch', greyscale via feColorMatrix) at opacity 0.04-0.08 on a body::before with pointer-events: none. Use it as a tiled background-image, not a live CSS filter on a full-screen element, which is expensive on phones. If a gradient is used, make it a barely perceptible two-stop tint in the same hue (linear-gradient(in oklab, ...)) behind the hero only. Inline data-URI keeps the zero-third-party promise.
- CONTACT AS THE ONE STRONG COLOUR MOMENT: the WhatsApp Contact action is the only filled, high-contrast button on any screen; every other link is text with an underline (text-decoration-thickness: 1px; text-underline-offset: 0.2em; thicker on hover). On mobile keep one persistent Contact bar at the bottom (min target 44x44px, WCAG 2.2 needs at least 24x24), respecting env(safe-area-inset-bottom), and ensure it never covers focused elements (scroll-padding-block-end equal to its height, SC 2.4.11). Keep the Faisal AI-assistant disclosure visibly adjacent to the phone number in the same block, in normal-contrast text, not as fine print.
- HEADER AND FOOTER AS TYPOGRAPHY: replace the two-row header with a single row: wordmark in the display serif, 5 nav links in 15px sans, language switch written in the other script (already done: 'English' / 'العربية'), Contact pill. Footer as a large-type colophon: the name set at display size across the full width, then three small columns (navigation, contact with Faisal disclosure, legal/privacy). This full-width name footer is a recognisable editorial signature that costs zero bytes.
- CONTAINER QUERIES FOR COMPONENTS: make cards, the stat row and the journey steps respond to their container (container-type: inline-size; @container (inline-size > 34rem)) so the same component works in the narrow sticky-label layout and full width (Chrome 105+, Firefox 110+, Safari 16+). If cqi units are used for type, combine with rem, e.g. clamp(1.25rem, 1rem + 2cqi, 2rem), per web.dev/articles/baseline-in-action-container-queries, so zoom still works.
- PORTRAIT TREATMENT: it is the only photograph, so use it once per page at most, at high quality (Astro <Image> with AVIF/WebP, explicit width/height, fetchpriority=high on the homepage only). The source is 990x977, so never display it wider than about 495 CSS px on 2x screens. Unify it with the palette by placing it on the tinted-neutral paper tone and keeping the single arch mask; do not add duotone filters, blobs or floating badges around it.
- FOCUS AND STATES AS DESIGN: a visible, branded focus ring is part of the editorial look: :focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--ink); outline-offset: 3px; } with at least 3:1 contrast against both paper and the filled button. Style ::selection in the accent tint and set scroll-margin-block-start on headings for the sticky header. These small details are what human-crafted sites have and generated ones lack.
- VERIFICATION LOOP: after each change run the existing screenshot helper at 1440 and 390 for /en/ and /ar/, check 375px for horizontal overflow (document.documentElement.scrollWidth <= innerWidth), test at 200% zoom, emulate prefers-reduced-motion, and view in Firefox (no text-wrap: pretty, no cross-document transitions, no interpolate-size) to confirm the fallback is still handsome.

Pitfalls:
- Research limits to know about: Firecrawl was out of credits, so research used Tavily search/extract, which returns page text, not rendering. Exemplar layout descriptions for Klim, Rauno and Stripe Press therefore lean on prior knowledge and were not visually verified in this session; wlrk.com (a text-only law-firm site I wanted as an exemplar) failed to load and is not cited. Browser-version figures come from caniuse pages opened today (2026-11-13).
- The generic 'AI-made' cluster (Paralect, 925studios): Inter for everything, indigo/purple gradient, three equal rounded cards with thin-line icons, an all-caps tracked eyebrow above every heading, one coloured italic serif word in each headline, a pill badge or dot above the title, the same radius and soft shadow on every element, light grey secondary text, and fade-up on every block. Any one is fine; three or more together read as a template. The current site already has two: an eyebrow label and a coloured second line in the hero. Keep each to one use per page.
- The serif-headline-plus-arch-portrait-plus-three-stats hero is itself now a common 'premium clinic' template. Differentiate by scale, asymmetry and the Arabic calligraphic display, not by adding more ornaments around it.
- Eyebrow labels that only name the section type ('Treatments', 'FAQ') add a line and no information. Use a label only when it carries data: a step number, a date reviewed, a category.
- Reveal-on-scroll that starts from opacity: 0 outside an @supports block, or driven by IntersectionObserver JS: fast scrollers, failed scripts, iOS 18 and print all see blank space. Default must be visible.
- Putting animation-timeline before the animation shorthand, which silently resets it to auto; and forgetting that a 'both' fill on a view() timeline can leave elements hidden when printing or in reader modes.
- Fluid type written as pure vw, or with px min/max, or with max more than 2.5x min: fails WCAG 1.4.4 at 200% zoom on wide screens. Also clamp() headlines that are huge on desktop but leave Arabic or long medical words overflowing at 375px; add overflow-wrap: anywhere on display headings and test the longest Arabic and English strings.
- Applying Latin styling to Arabic: letter-spacing on eyebrows and buttons breaks the joins; italics do not exist; uppercase is meaningless; Latin line-height 1.4 clips marks; identical px size makes Arabic look smaller; text-box-trim with cap edges cuts diacritics. Also Amiri is a heavy download, so do not load weights or styles that are not used and keep the Arabic page from growing past its current roughly 470 KB.
- Mirroring by hand with left/right, translateX or absolute offsets instead of logical properties; forgetting transform-origin and gradient directions flip; mirroring icons that should not flip; letting the phone number reorder in RTL.
- Decoration that needs a caption to make sense (labelled abstract shapes), gradient text, glassmorphism cards, glow, background grids, noise at more than about 10% opacity, and live CSS/SVG filters over the full viewport (jank on mid-range Android).
- Trusting oklch lightness for contrast: two colours with a 0.4 lightness gap can still fail 4.5:1. Measure. Also provide sRGB fallbacks before oklch/color-mix declarations for older Samsung Internet and Safari.
- Shipping the Newsreader variable file with both axes and italic without subsetting can add 200-450 KB and wreck the 250 KB budget; conversely shipping a build without opsz makes large headings look clumsy. Decide deliberately and preload only the one font used above the fold.
- View-transition traps: duplicate view-transition-name values on a page cancel the whole transition; long durations (over about 250ms) make a static site feel slower; not gating on prefers-reduced-motion. Never add a JS router to get transitions.
- Exclusive accordions and collapsed-by-default medical content hide sourced answers and risks from scanning, from find-in-page outside Chromium, and from print. Keep risks and key facts in the open flow; use details only for the FAQ list, and add a print stylesheet that expands them.
- Sticky elements that fail silently because an ancestor has overflow: hidden/auto, or that cover focused controls on mobile (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured).
- Design moves that would break the owner's rules and must not be imported from luxury or clinic sites: testimonial or review carousels, before/after sliders, logo walls or 'as seen in' strips, invented counters ('5,000+ operations'), award badges, star ratings, superlative headlines, 'book now' buttons, maps and opening hours, stock operating-room or patient imagery, AI-generated people, robot product renders or vendor names, and any form that invites uploading reports. Marquee tickers and count-up number animations also read as promotional and should be avoided.
- Rewriting approved copy to make line breaks or pull quotes fit. Typography must adapt to the fixed wording (use text-wrap: balance, max-inline-size in ch, and per-language overrides), never the reverse.


### arabic-rtl
- What I saw on the current Arabic pages: the base is already sound (IBM Plex Sans Arabic body at 18px/1.85, letter-spacing 0 and no uppercase on Arabic eyebrows, italics off, logical properties). The weak point is Amiri for h1/h2. At 60-77px it reads as a classical book or Quranic face, with hairline strokes and very loose word gaps ('ليس كل مريض'), next to the modern Plex body. It is also the reason the Arabic homepage is about 220 KB heavier than the English one.
- Recommended Arabic stack (A): keep IBM Plex Sans Arabic for body and UI, the Saudi government standard. Replace Amiri with Markazi Text variable (@fontsource-variable/markazi-text, Arabic subset 41 KB for weights 400-700). It is a moderate-contrast modern Naskh with its own matching Latin serif, so it sits naturally beside Newsreader. Arabic font payload drops from about 296 KB to about 130 KB. Markazi Text is drawn compact and looks small for its nominal size: expect to raise Arabic headings roughly 15-25% and confirm by screenshot.
- Alternative (B), the Vision 2030 / NEOM look: Arabic headings in Alexandria variable (31 KB) or Readex Pro variable (23 KB) at weight 300-500, body in Plex Arabic. These are the nearest open-source relatives of 29LT Bukra. The cost is that Arabic headings become sans while English stays serif, so the two languages get different personalities. Choose this only if the English display face also changes.
- Alternatives (C) and (D): (C) Noto Naskh Arabic variable (94 KB) is sturdier and plainer than Amiri and a safe fallback if Markazi looks too compact. (D) Thmanyah Serif Display would be the most recognisably Saudi editorial choice, but it is not on fontsource, is not OFL, and needs the owner to download it and confirm the licence covers self-hosting. Do not ship it without that.
- Fontsource facts (npm-verified, Arabic subset sizes measured). Variable packages exist for Noto Naskh Arabic, Noto Kufi Arabic (124 KB), Noto Sans Arabic (166 KB), Readex Pro, Alexandria, Cairo (31 KB), El Messiri (22 KB), Reem Kufi (12 KB), Markazi Text, Rubik (32 KB), Vazirmatn (46 KB), Mada, Changa, Kufam and Beiruti. Static only: IBM Plex Sans Arabic (about 43-46 KB per weight), Amiri (100-109 KB per weight), Tajawal (9 KB per weight), Almarai (32 KB), Zain (30 KB), Aref Ruqaa, Lateef (62-68 KB), Scheherazade New (79-89 KB), Harmattan (132-136 KB). Not on fontsource: Noto Serif Arabic (no such family), Noto Naskh Arabic UI, Thmanyah, the Saudi Font. Noto Nastaliq exists only as noto-nastaliq-urdu.
- Faces to avoid here. Noto Nastaliq and Gulzar are Urdu/Persian style and wrong for Saudi readers. Scheherazade New, Lateef and Harmattan are heavy, built for extended African and Asian orthographies, and have very tall line boxes. Aref Ruqaa, Rakkas, Lemonada, Lalezar, Marhey and Reem Kufi are decorative or informal and wrong for a surgeon. Rubik's rounded Arabic is too playful. Vazirmatn is Persian-first. Cairo and Tajawal are competent, but they are the default of countless Arabic templates and will not signal premium (my judgement, not a sourced finding).
- Headline style rule: Naskh or modern Naskh-serif for anything longer than a few words. Geometric or Kufi-hybrid only for short display lines such as hero titles, section numbers and labels. Never set paragraphs in Kufi. Saudi premium brands use monoline hybrids (Bukra) or modern Naskh serifs (Zarid) for headlines, not classical calligraphic revivals.
- Sizes: set Arabic about 10-15% larger than the Latin equivalent, because Arabic has no capitals and a small eye; Apple's guidance is about +2pt beside uppercase Latin. Practical floors for Arabic: body 17-18px, secondary text 15-16px, and nothing below 14px (the current .brand-title at 0.85rem, about 13.6px, is too small). Hero h1: clamp(2.5rem, 6vw, 4.5rem) for a full-size Naskh, larger for Markazi Text.
- Line-height for Arabic: body 1.75-1.9 (keep the current 1.85); lead paragraphs 1.7; h3 and cards 1.5; h1/h2 1.3-1.45, never below 1.25 or shadda and kasra marks clip or collide. Latin headings can stay at 1.05-1.15. For reference DGA uses 72/90, 48/60, 36/44, 18/28 and 16/24. Add extra padding-block to Arabic buttons and chips (about 0.1-0.15em more at the bottom) so descenders and dots are not cramped; check 'تواصل عبر واتساب'.
- Never track Arabic: letter-spacing must be 0 on every :lang(ar) element, including eyebrows, labels, buttons, nav and inputs. Negative tracking breaks joins just as positive tracking does. The DGA scale's -2% on large display sizes is a Latin habit; do not apply it to Arabic. A safe global rule is :lang(ar), :lang(ar) * { letter-spacing: 0 !important; text-transform: none }. To get the 'small caps eyebrow' effect in Arabic use weight 500-600, accent colour and a short rule or dot, not spacing. Kashida elongation (ـ) is the authentic equivalent, but it corrupts search and screen-reader output if typed into real text, so avoid it or confine it to an aria-hidden decorative copy.
- No italics, no faux bold, no translucent text in Arabic. Emphasise with colour or weight. Load every weight you use (Plex Arabic 400 and 600; add 500 only if needed) and set font-synthesis: none. Use solid hex colours for Arabic text rather than opacity or rgba, because overlapping joins can render darker. Links: text-decoration-thickness 1px, text-underline-offset at least 0.3em, text-decoration-skip-ink auto, or the existing background-line technique, so the underline clears the dots under ي ب ج.
- Weights: Arabic bolds are optically lighter than Latin bolds. Use 600 where English uses 500-600 for UI and avoid thin weights of 300 or less below about 28px. For display in a Naskh serif, weight 400-500 is enough; 700 on large Naskh looks heavy and dated.
- Numerals: use Western digits (0-9) everywhere in Arabic and never mix systems on a page. W3C lists Saudi Arabia as an Arabic-Indic region, but DGA components, the Vision 2030 guideline and Saudi phone and WhatsApp conventions all show Western digits, and medical values (BMI, kg, cm, years) are read that way. Use font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining-nums on stats and the BMI result. Keep '%' and '+' attached to the number inside an isolated LTR span so they cannot jump sides.
- BMI inputs: Saudi iOS and Android keyboards may type ٠-٩ depending on the user's settings. Use inputmode=decimal and normalise Arabic-Indic digits and the Arabic decimal separator (٫) to ASCII before calculating. This is a real usability gap for Arabic-first users.
- Bidi hygiene: wrap every phone number, URL, email, acronym (FACS, ASMBS, IFSO, BMI) and Latin proper name inside Arabic text in <bdi> or <span dir="ltr" lang="en">, with unicode-bidi: isolate. For the English-titled source citations under the 15 FAQs on the Arabic page, give each item lang="en" dir="ltr" but keep the list's start edge on the right; short one-to-two-line items follow page direction and paragraphs of three or more lines align by their own language (Apple). The language switcher shows each language in its own script ('English' on the Arabic page, 'العربية' on the English page), each with its own lang attribute.
- Mirror in RTL: page grid and column order (text on the right, portrait on the left, as now), nav order, breadcrumbs, the journey steps and any timeline or progress line (they run right to left), 'next/continue' arrows and chevrons (point left), list markers and indents, quote marks and borders, decorative offsets, hover nudges, the accordion chevron side, and the linear BMI scale if drawn as a bar (low on the right, high on the left). Implement with logical properties only (margin-inline, padding-inline, inset-inline, border-inline, text-align: start) and a single transform: scaleX(-1) for directional SVGs.
- Do not mirror: the portrait photo (never flip a face); the WhatsApp and phone glyphs and any logo; checkmarks, plus/minus and close icons; clock or duration icons; slashes; the digit order inside numbers and phone numbers; Latin acronyms; anatomical or medical line art where left and right carry meaning (a mirrored stomach diagram is anatomically wrong); charts with a numeric axis.
- Arabic text layout: text-align: start, never justify (browsers justify Arabic by stretching spaces only, which gives rivers). No hyphens and no word-break: break-all; use overflow-wrap: break-word only for URLs. Arabic text is denser, so hold the measure at 34-40rem (about 60-70 characters). Add text-wrap: balance on Arabic headings and text-wrap: pretty on paragraphs to avoid one-word last lines. Arabic has no abbreviations or initial caps, so do not design components that rely on truncated labels or drop caps.
- Arabic as first-class: write Arabic-specific rules for every component rather than inheriting Latin metrics. Consider an Arabic monogram (for example 'ع' or 'د. ع') instead of the Latin 'AH' on Arabic pages. Check that Latin-only tokens in an Arabic layout (FACS, +15, ASMBS) are set in the Latin face with matched optical size (Latin about 0.9em inside Arabic lines), not in an Arabic font's fallback Latin. Review every headline for line breaks in Arabic; do not accept the English break positions.
- Saudi visual conventions that signal quality: lots of white space and few elements per screen; very dark neutral text (#0D121C to #161F2B) on white or warm off-white; one deep, slightly desaturated brand colour, usually green in the #14573A-#1B8354 range, with teal, navy and lavender also accepted; a restrained metallic or sand accent (gold near #DBA102, used at under 5% of area); thin 1px rules; modest radii; fine-line geometric or Najdi-inspired patterns as low-contrast texture; bilingual parity; official credential lines such as licence or classification numbers set plainly. Loud gradients, stock imagery and crowded sliders read as low-end clinic marketing.
- Performance: Arabic fonts are the main weight driver. Load at most three Arabic files (Plex Arabic 400 and 600, plus one variable display file). Preload only the Arabic body 400 file and the display file on /ar/ pages and nothing Arabic on /en/. Use font-display: swap with a metric-matched fallback (size-adjust and ascent-override against Geeza Pro, Segoe UI or Noto) to prevent layout shift, since Arabic line boxes differ a lot between fonts. The Amiri imports can then be dropped from Base.astro and 404.astro.

Pitfalls:
- Verification limits. design.dga.gov.sa could not be fetched from this sandbox (connection errors on two tools); DGA numbers come from community mirrors and search excerpts and should be re-checked against the official site before being quoted as official. No Saudi hospital site (KFSHRC, HMG, Seha, MoH) was opened, so there are no verified findings about hospital typography. The size increase needed for Markazi Text is an estimate, not a measurement.
- Do not imitate government identity too closely: no gov.sa-style verification bar, DGA digital stamp, state emblem (palm and swords), Vision 2030 logo or exact Platforms Code green-on-white component styling. The Vision 2030 guideline says its mark must reflect a national initiative, not private profit, and a private doctor's site that looks official could also read as a misleading credential.
- Do not use the licensed fonts the premium brands use (29LT Bukra, 29LT Zarid, DIN Next Arabic, GE SS, Sakkal Majalla) from unofficial download sites; they are widely pirated. Thmanyah and the Saudi Font are free but not OFL and not on npm; they need a licence check and a manual, owner-approved download.
- Letter-spacing or text-transform leaking into Arabic through a new component (eyebrows, badges, buttons, table headers, form labels). The current CSS overrides each one by hand; a redesign that adds components will reintroduce the bug unless there is a global :lang(ar) reset.
- Tight Latin heading line-heights (1.0-1.15) inherited by Arabic h1/h2/h3, card titles or stats, which clips shadda and kasra and makes stacked lines collide. Check words with stacked marks such as 'بدقّة' and 'يُحدَّد'.
- Kufi or geometric display faces used for paragraphs, FAQ answers or risk explanations. Type designers call Kufi in long text an abuse of Arabic typography, and it hurts the site's main job of clear patient education.
- Flipping everything with one blanket transform or a global [dir=rtl] svg rule. That mirrors the portrait, the WhatsApp glyph, checkmarks, digits and medical illustrations. Flip only icons explicitly tagged as directional.
- Phone number or '+966' rendering reversed or with the plus on the wrong side; '+15' or '15+' and '%' drifting; brackets around Latin terms flipping. Each needs explicit dir="ltr" isolation and a visual test in the Arabic build, including the sticky mobile contact bar and the tel: and wa.me links.
- Mixing Arabic-Indic and Western digits, for example dates from Intl with the ar-SA locale, which defaults to Arabic-Indic digits and the Hijri calendar. If any date is formatted in JS, use the 'ar-SA-u-nu-latn-ca-gregory' locale or hard-code the strings.
- Typing tatweel (ـ) into headings for a justified or 'designed' look. It changes the underlying text, hurting search matching and screen-reader pronunciation, and it breaks on reflow.
- Treating Arabic as a translation layer: reusing English line breaks, English measure widths, Latin-sized small print (12-13px Arabic is not comfortably legible) or Latin-only decorative devices such as drop caps, small caps and italic pull quotes with no Arabic equivalent designed.
- Font swaps that silently raise weight or cause layout shift. Noto Sans Arabic variable is 166 KB and Noto Kufi 124 KB for the Arabic subset alone. Heavy Naskh files plus Latin subsets can push the Arabic homepage beyond today's 470 KB. Measure after any change.
- Underlines, focus rings and text-box cropping tuned on Latin. Arabic descenders (ج ع ي) and dots sit lower, so underline offsets, outline-offset and any fixed-height pills must be checked in Arabic at 375px and at 200% zoom for WCAG 1.4.4, 1.4.10 and 1.4.12. Text-spacing overrides must not break the layout, even though letter-spacing itself should stay 0 by default.
- Using the recommended deep green or gold only decoratively is fine. Do not add crests, 'accredited' seals, ministry logos or Vision 2030 marks, and do not pair gold with superlatives. The site's own rules forbid invented credentials and MoH advertising rules forbid superlatives.
